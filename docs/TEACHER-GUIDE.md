# Teacher guide: version 1.2

Every activity follows the same rhythm: **Learn it** (short explanation and diagram), **Try it** (the game), **Check it** (questions with a hint ladder), **Self-rate** (proficiency scale, formative only). Turn on **Teacher** view to see answers and teacher notes, and **projector mode** for whole-class demos.

| Activity | Syllabus | Game style | Suggested lesson use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **PPC Explorer** | 1.1 PPC (choice, opportunity cost, unemployment, actual growth, growth in production possibilities, increasing vs constant opportunity cost) | Strategy missions on an island economy, plus event scenarios. **Four seasons**: predict each season's event, then meet the island's need for fish and timber | **Hook (10 min, projector):** play the event scenarios as a class. Students show 1 to 5 fingers to predict the outcome before you play each scenario. **Pairs (15 min):** complete the missions, then compare the opportunity cost bars in increasing and constant mode. **Exit:** the ice storm "Explain it in writing" task. |
| **Castaway Council** | 1.1 Scarcity, factors of production, opportunity cost, free goods, basic economic questions, economic systems | Island story. **Level 1**: sort what washes ashore into the factors of production, then name the opportunity cost of the council's choice. **Level 2**: run three villages as a free market, planned and mixed economy. **Level 3**: name the economic system from detective clues | **Hook (projector):** the class votes on each find before a student taps. **Debate:** students with different project rankings compare their opportunity costs. **Exit:** one village's strength and weakness. |
| **Money River** | 1.1 Circular flow of income: households, firms, banks, government, foreign sector; leakages and injections | River builder with animated coins. **Level 1**: build the two-sector flow. **Level 2**: place the drains (leakages) and springs (injections). **Level 3**: forecast whether national income rises, falls or stays the same | **Demo:** build the river on the projector, then ask what happens if households save more. **Pairs:** level 3 forecasts with reasons written before checking. |
| **Fact Lab** | 1.2 Positive and normative economics, value judgements, the economic method (hypothesis, ceteris paribus, evidence, refutation), economic thought from the 18th to 21st century | Lab conveyor belt. **Level 1**: stamp statements positive or normative, with traps. **Level 2**: find the value word. **Level 3**: order the method, rewrite opinions as testable claims, build the timeline of ideas | **Starter:** thumbs up or down for each statement on the belt. **TOK link:** does the positive and normative split exist in other subjects? |
| **Market Shock Simulator** | 2.1 to 2.3 Demand, supply, non-price determinants, movements vs shifts, equilibrium, price mechanism. HL: why demand slopes down | Prediction game in rounds of 8, with a trap mode. A queue of buyers or a pile of unsold bags shrinks as the price adjusts | **Starter:** five scenarios on the projector; students write their prediction on a mini whiteboard first. **Practice:** individual play with **trap mode on** to fix the shift vs movement confusion. **Check:** the price mechanism question after each scenario (signalling, incentive, rationing). |
| **Surplus Shader** | 2.3 Consumer, producer and community surplus, allocative efficiency (MB = MC), welfare loss. HL: calculating surplus areas | Price dial lab with challenges. **Paint the surplus**: students paint CS, PS or welfare loss and get a match score | **Demo:** drag the price above and below equilibrium on the projector and ask "who loses, who gains?". **Pairs:** challenges. **HL:** each student solves two "Calculate it" problems and shows working (½ × base × height) in their notes before checking. |
| **Mind Tricks Lab** (HL) | 2.4 HL only. Rational consumer choice assumptions; biases (rule of thumb, anchoring, framing, availability), bounded rationality, bounded self-control, bounded selfishness, imperfect information; default, restricted and mandated choice; nudge theory; business objectives (profit maximization, CSR, market share, satisficing, growth) | Experiment lab. **Level 1**: take part in five quick experiments, then a curtain reveals the trick and students name the bias. **Level 2**: spot the bias or limit in everyday scenarios. **Level 3**: design a nudge for four policy goals and say why it works, then match firms to their objectives | **Projected:** run the yogurt and lucky wheel experiments with a show of hands before the reveal (nothing is saved or sent). **Debate:** is a default choice fair, or does it exploit biases? |
| **The Elasticity Café** | 2.5 PED, PED and total revenue, determinants of PED (HINTS) | Business simulation with hidden PED, customers and a till. **Level 1**: grow revenue and say if demand is elastic or inelastic. **Level 2**: find the price that earns the most revenue (where \|PED\| = 1). **Level 3**: work out each day's PED before the café opens | **Split the room:** half run the latte café (elastic), half the ski lodge hot chocolate (inelastic). Compare sales logs, then link results to HINTS. **Debrief:** why did the same price rise raise revenue for one café and lower it for the other? |
| **Same Slope, Different PED** | 2.5 HL PED along a straight-line demand curve, PED and TR. SL: perfectly elastic, perfectly inelastic and unitary curves | Explorer with discovery challenges. **Mystery**: PED and TR are hidden; students calculate them to find a hidden point. Fewer checks and a new curve at levels 2 and 3 | **HL follow-up to the café:** students find |PED| = 1 and the TR peak, then explain the title in one sentence. **SL classes:** use the "special demand curves" section only. |
| **HINTS Market** | 2.5 Determinants of PED (HINTS), YED, Engel curves, normal, luxury, necessity and inferior goods. HL: why primary commodities have a lower PED than manufactured products | Market stall. **Level 1**: goods slide down a chute; pick the HINTS letter and the elastic or inelastic crate. **Level 2**: raise income and watch Engel curves draw themselves, then classify each good. **Level 3**: calculate PED and YED at the calculator corner | **Starter:** project level 1 and let the class shout the HINTS letter. **Pairs:** level 2, then compare a luxury and an inferior good's Engel curve. |
| **Supply Speed** | 2.6 PES, its determinants (time, unused capacity, ability to store stock, factor mobility, how fast costs rise), the momentary period, short run and long run. HL: primary commodities vs manufactured products | Producer race. **Level 1**: rank three producers from least to most elastic supply, then name the determinant. **Level 2**: travel through time with one producer and watch the supply curve flatten. **Level 3**: calculate and classify PES | **Demo:** the strawberry farm, bakery and app studio race on the projector. **Exit:** one PES calculation with working shown. |
| **Government Toolkit** | 2.7 Maximum and minimum prices, indirect (specific) taxes, subsidies, stakeholders. HL: tax incidence and calculations | Minister's missions: read a brief, pick the right tool, set its size to meet a goal. **Level 2**: predict the effects first. **Level 3 (HL)**: calculate revenue, burden, welfare loss and more | **Pairs:** level 1 missions, then argue for the stakeholder who loses most. **HL:** level 3 missions with working shown in notes before checking. | **HL follow-up to the café:** students find |PED| = 1 and the TR peak, then explain the title in one sentence. **SL classes:** use the "special demand curves" section only. |
| **Smoke and Sunshine** | 2.8 Negative and positive externalities of production and consumption, MPC, MSC, MPB, MSB, welfare loss, merit and demerit goods, government responses. HL: calculating welfare loss | Town fixer. **Level 1**: name each spillover and its diagram gap. **Level 2**: choose a policy and set its size to reach Q*; the smoke clears as output gets close. **Level 3 (HL)**: calculate the welfare loss, then judge the policy | **Hook:** show the smoky town and ask what is wrong. **Pairs:** level 2, then debate which policy works best for the cement factory. **HL:** level 3 with working in notes. |
| **Fish Pond** | 2.8 Common pool resources: rivalrous but non-excludable, the tragedy of the commons, threats to sustainability, quotas, tradable permits, legislation, collective self-governance, international agreements | Shared pond. **Level 1**: fish for 8 seasons with three other boats; can the stock survive? **Level 2**: try a rule (quota, permits, self-governance, licence, international agreement) and watch the stock chart. **Level 3**: keep the pond healthy when one fisher cheats | **Whole class:** project level 1 and let the class vote on the catch each season. **Debrief:** why did holding back alone not save the pond? |
| **Streetlight Fund** | 2.9 Public goods: non-rivalry, non-excludability, the free rider problem, direct provision and contracting out | Neighbourhood game. **Level 1**: sort goods on a rival and excludable grid. **Level 2**: give to the streetlight fund with four neighbours and watch free riding grow; the lights fail until the council steps in. **Level 3**: choose direct provision or contracting out and set the tax per household | **Hook:** play level 2 on the projector and ask who free rode. **Exit:** why can't a private firm sell streetlights? |

## Levels, stamps and sound (updated in 1.2)

- **Three levels per game.** Each game has a level picker. Level 1 is open. Level 2 opens when the student earns the level 1 stamp, and level 3 opens with the level 2 stamp. In teacher view every level is open.
- **Five stamps per activity:** one for each level, **First-Try Star** (every Check it question right on the first try) and **Full Circle** (all four steps done). Students see them on the home page and in **Stamps**. Stamps are personal, never taken away and never ranked. They travel in the progress code and appear on the summary image. Old progress codes still load.
- **One free hint.** Using one hint on a question still counts as a first try. A second hint, or a wrong answer, does not.
- **Stamps need the economics.** Each game stamp asks for a right prediction, explanation or calculation, not just play.

## Class mode (Market Shock, on the projector)

Open Teacher view, open Market Shock, then choose **Class mode (projector)**. Name 2 to 6 teams. For each of 8 scenarios, teams discuss and hold up their answer; you tap each team's answer, then **Reveal the answer**. A diagram and explanation appear. Every right answer adds to **one class score**, so teams help the class rather than compete. Nothing is saved.

## Class link (turn activities on for your class without editing files)

In Teacher view, find **Class link**. Choose the activities your class should see, choose whether to show HL content, then copy the link or show the QR code. When a student opens the link, the app asks before using your settings on their device. Make a new link when you release more activities. On your own device, **Stop using it on this device** goes back to the normal settings.

## Tips

- **Release activities as you reach each topic.** Set them to `false` in `config/settings.json` until then (see the README, section 5).
- **Self-rate is honest.** The app suggests a level from first-try answers, hints used and the harder "apply it" questions. Exemplary needs every "apply it" question right with no hints. Students still choose their own rating; use it to start a conversation, not as a mark.
- **Hint ladder.** Hint 1 nudges, hint 2 narrows, then a worked example of a similar problem. The answer only appears after all three.
- **Written answers** in "Explain it in writing" save on the student's own device as they type, and appear under **My writing** in My progress. They are never sent and are not in the progress code. Ask students to copy them into your learning platform.
- **Check it diagrams.** Every activity has at least one "label the diagram" question, where students choose the label for each lettered part.
- **Progress summary.** Students can download a one-page image of their progress and hand it in on your learning platform. The app sends nothing.
- **Switching devices.** Share the student help sheet (docs/STUDENT-HELP.md). Students use a progress code or QR code.
- **Mistakes in content?** Every word is in `content/`. See docs/EDITING-CONTENT.md.
