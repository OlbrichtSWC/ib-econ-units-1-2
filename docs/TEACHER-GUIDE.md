# Teacher guide: version 1.1

Every activity follows the same rhythm: **Learn it** (short explanation and diagram), **Try it** (the game), **Check it** (questions with a hint ladder), **Self-rate** (proficiency scale, formative only). Turn on **Teacher** view to see answers and teacher notes, and **projector mode** for whole-class demos.

| Activity | Syllabus | Game style | Suggested lesson use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **PPC Explorer** | 1.1 PPC (choice, opportunity cost, unemployment, actual growth, growth in production possibilities, increasing vs constant opportunity cost) | Strategy missions on an island economy, plus event cards. **Four seasons**: predict each season's event, then meet the island's need for fish and timber | **Hook (10 min, projector):** play the event cards as a class. Students show 1 to 5 fingers to predict the outcome before you play each card. **Pairs (15 min):** complete the missions, then compare the opportunity cost bars in increasing and constant mode. **Exit:** the ice storm "Explain it in writing" task. |
| **Market Shock Simulator** | 2.1 to 2.3 Demand, supply, non-price determinants, movements vs shifts, equilibrium, price mechanism. HL: why demand slopes down | Prediction card game in rounds of 8, with a trap mode. A queue of buyers or a pile of unsold bags shrinks as the price adjusts | **Starter:** five cards on the projector; students write their prediction on a mini whiteboard first. **Practice:** individual play with **trap mode on** to fix the shift vs movement confusion. **Check:** the price mechanism question after each card (signalling, incentive, rationing). |
| **Surplus Shader** | 2.3 Consumer, producer and community surplus, allocative efficiency (MB = MC), welfare loss. HL: calculating surplus areas | Price dial lab with challenges. **Paint the surplus**: students paint CS, PS or welfare loss and get a match score | **Demo:** drag the price above and below equilibrium on the projector and ask "who loses, who gains?". **Pairs:** challenges. **HL:** each student solves two "Calculate it" cards and shows working (½ × base × height) in their notes before checking. |
| **The Elasticity Café** | 2.5 PED, PED and total revenue, determinants of PED (HINTS) | Business simulation with hidden PED, customers and a till. **4-week campaign**: four cafés; the goal each week is to end with more daily revenue than day 1 | **Split the room:** half run the latte café (elastic), half the ski lodge hot chocolate (inelastic). Compare sales logs, then link results to HINTS. **Debrief:** why did the same price rise raise revenue for one café and lower it for the other? |
| **Same Slope, Different PED** | 2.5 HL PED along a straight-line demand curve, PED and TR. SL: perfectly elastic, perfectly inelastic and unitary curves | Explorer with discovery challenges. **Mystery**: PED and TR are hidden; students calculate them to find a hidden point | **HL follow-up to the café:** students find PED = 1 and the TR peak, then explain the title in one sentence. **SL classes:** use the "special demand curves" section only. |

## Stamps and sound (new in 1.1)

- **Stamps.** Each activity has three stamps: one for its game goal, **First-Try Star** (every Check it question right on the first try, no hints) and **Full Circle** (all four steps done). Students see them on the home page and in **Stamps**. Stamps are personal, never taken away and never ranked. They travel in the progress code and appear on the summary image.
- **Game goals:** Island Planner (meet all four seasons' needs), Market Mover (6 of 8 right in a round), Surplus Painter (3 areas painted at 85% or better), Café Tycoon (revenue grows in 3 of 4 campaign weeks), Point Hunter (find 3 hidden points).
- **Sound** is on by default and quiet. Students switch it with **Sound on / Sound off** in the top bar. In a lab, ask students to turn it off or use headphones.
- Devices set to reduce motion get no confetti or animations.

## Tips

- **Release activities as you reach each topic.** Set them to `false` in `config/settings.json` until then (see the README, section 5).
- **Self-rate is honest.** The app suggests a level from first-try answers, hints used and the harder "apply it" questions. Exemplary needs every "apply it" question right with no hints. Students still choose their own rating; use it to start a conversation, not as a mark.
- **Hint ladder.** Hint 1 nudges, hint 2 narrows, then a worked example of a similar problem. The answer only appears after all three.
- **Written answers** in "Explain it in writing" are never saved. Ask students to copy them into their notes or your learning platform.
- **Progress summary.** Students can download a one-page image of their progress and hand it in on your learning platform. The app sends nothing.
- **Switching devices.** Share the student help sheet (docs/STUDENT-HELP.md). Students use a progress code or QR code.
- **Mistakes in content?** Every word is in `content/`. See docs/EDITING-CONTENT.md.
